### The story of me and writing

I spent the greater part of my life, until now, thinking that writing was something quite boring. I simply didn't see any reason to do it. I thought that other forms of expression, like the visual arts or even video games, were much more interesting and worthwhile; furthermore, I was convinced that these areas were where my skills lied anyways. These prejudices were never challenged until I came to Canada about four years and went to a public high school. Until then, for reasons I won't get into here, I had never truly been exposed to writing or even taught to write at all. (It may sound funny, but at the age of fourteen, I was only able to, very haltingly, write in print with all capital letters. And I couldn't even spell the most basic of words like "comes" or "goes".) I learned how to read very late, and simply being able to read was enough for me. At the time, I never felt the need to write anything down. Interestingly, I had a great many ideas about a great many things, but I somehow thought that my mind was some sort of steel trap. I can practically hear the youth talking! With age, I learned that ideas accumulate faster than birthdays, which already seem to be coming and going too fast. Even after becoming completely competent in the art of writing, I still only saw it as just something I did for school - something that I did for the world and not for myself. But thoughts continued to accumulate. Usually my ideas related to things that I wanted to create in the future. I would occasionally write these things down, often times accompanying the words with little drawings or sketches. But I still saw the writing as the secondary form of communication and not the first. It was only ever something put there as an afterthought because I lacked the time to fulfil my whole vision. This pattern continued for far longer than one would think before I started to realize the true value of written language. I started to realize that there was simply no other method more efficient for communicating a large amount of thoughts in a quick manner. Suddenly, writing was more than just my pathway to a diploma; it was something that I did for me. I had finally seen this craft for all of its quiet potential. My ideas now had a place to live, and, as many others before me have realized, I saw that my ideas and thoughts only grew in value as I wrote them down. From this point on, I was hooked. I only kept writing more and more, until I realized that writing wasn't just something I did to communicate ideas: it was an artform I had fallen in love with! This is the end of the story, and I must apologize for going on for too long. But let this paragraph be a testament to my love for this art.
