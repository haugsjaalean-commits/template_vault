I once watched a YouTube video whos title was something like "The point of Obsidian isn't Obsidian". The main point of the video was that the pont of Obsidian wasn't really the features it offered but the form in which they came. The point of Obsidian is that all the files are stored locally on the computer with completely plain text. On top of all of that, Obsidian also has a public API. This means that any one can build more or less anything they want on top of Obsidian. 

To me, the point of Obsidian isn't that it does a lot of useful things which allow me to organize my files, because it doesn't. What it does have is a really strong base of essential features. The most powerful of which is surely the `.base`. Not only do bases query the entire vault, but they also have a public API. This is truly incredible. Features like `.base` files are what make Obsidian worth it. 

The way I see things now, Obsidian isn't even worth the time if you do not plan on massively customizing it. It simply doesn't offer very much in terms of features, but, with tools like Claude Code, creating plugins has never been easier. Obsidian invites the creation of plugins, and I see this as the whole point of the app. Obsidian is simply an excellently elegant and simple base onto which we can build whatever we want. Now that I look at things this way, it really feels like Obsidian was built for this. It was never meant to be something that people used to organize things; it was meant to be the basis of more advanced systems.
