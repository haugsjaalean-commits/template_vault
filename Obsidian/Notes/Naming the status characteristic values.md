## Thoughts

Honestly I think that my original system is the best. I thought that `idea` was a bad idea because calling a bug an idea felt really wrong to me. After thinking about this more, it's clear that a bug is not a project. A *bug fix* is a project. This means that a bug fix is just something that talks about a bug. If I only just noticed a bug, then the fix for the bug is just an idea. This makes perfect sense and I don't see any contradiction. A project is, at the heart of it, the act of turning an idea into a reality, so not using "idea" would be a bad choice. 

## More thoughts


`seen`, `mapped`, `started`, `attained`, `in progress`, `abandoned`, `redirected`

All of these suggest some sort of journey. I imagine a traveler seeing a far away peak or monument. At this point the goal of the project has been seen; it is on the horizon as something that the traveler can move towards. Next, the traveler starts to produce a map. They think about the obstacles ahead and the things that can help them move forwards. They chart their path and make their plan. It is at this point that they start their journey. They make their way to their objective - all the while checking their map to make sure that they are on chart. Sometimes, they realize that their original idea of how to move forwards was erroneous, so they have to back track and go back to mapmaking. After continuing this process for some time, the weary traveler may attain their objective. However, we must be careful here, as not all objectives are as close as they seem at first. The traveler will often reach false peaks on their journey. When this happens, they will be forced to wonder if the true objective is even something that they can attain, or if it is something they will be moving towards until the very day they collapse. 

Of course, not all journeys are meant to be finished or kill you trying; some journeys are just things we do for a while before we decide that there are other things that deserve more attention. When this is the case, we may abandon the effort completely and walk back down the mountain. However, giving up isn't always the only option. If the traveler realizes that the place they were trying to get to was not what it seemed, or that another location would actually better suit this journey. In this case, they may decide to divert their energy and travel somewhere different. 

### Thoughts on the thoughts

(This is kind of like the explanation in the beginning of my version of the [[Tao To Ching]].) I think that it is interesting to think of these different flow charts as being represented by different real-world things. Not only does it help us contextualize the meaning of the different categories and understand what we are even saying about the note, but it also allows us create a story. Creating a story is important because, if we stop to think about it, saying that something is an "idea", for example, doesn't actually mean much at all. What is an idea versus a draft? Answering that question is not as obvious as it seems I think. At first I thought that the words I employed in these flow charts held great importance, and they do, just not in the way I had initially thought. I realize now that is not the specific words that matter, but instead how the words work together to, as I said, create a story. An idea is actually a very vague thing, and I think that it would be very challenging indeed to attempt to draw the line between idea and implementation or furthermore idea and plan. I have theorized that ideas are simply the mind using its capacity to understand language in order to recreate faux realities for itself. When we look at things this way, it becomes even more confusing to think about what an *idea* could possibly be. That's why I think it is so important that we ground ourselves in a real world situation like a journey through the physical world. Instead of worrying about what "idea" means, we can simply see things in a more relational or topographical manner. We can ask ourselves, "Where does the current stage exist compared to the others?".
