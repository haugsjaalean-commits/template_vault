### My writing



*Life After Cable* is about the often unseen harms of constant distraction. Even though it was written decades ago, the article has probably never been more relavent. Just replace the word "cable" with "internet" and we would have an article that could appear in any modern journal. Our team conversation revolved heavily around the idea that the internet has undeniably (and seemingly irreversibly) changed the way we live in society. One of us even described our phones as being literal extensions of our arms. I think that this is a potent way of describing the effect that smartphones have had on us. It was clear to us that this atomic connection with our portable devices has separated us from each other. The interactions we have on social media seem to be more present in our lives than even our interactions with our family members. And even when we are out living our lives, our thoughts may be influenced by the idea of social media - the idea that we need to look good for posts or that we should even be posting in the first place. In this way, social media has made us value authenticity all the more.

The number of people dependent on the internet to fulfil both material and social needs is only growing. As I see it, this is in great part a result of capitalisme. The continued functioning of society depends on fulfilling the wishes of investors. I may even go as far as to say that society is split into two groups: investors and investments. The vast majority of human beings have been reduced to investments for the investors. An investment shouldn't need to have real social interactions. An investment just needs to sit there and continue doing its job. Every person has their purpose, and everything outside of that is considered indulgence. That is the reason for the continued spread of loneliness and lack of community. Being human is an indulgence...
