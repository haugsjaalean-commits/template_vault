I was reading [this wiki article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity), and  it talked about how technological advancements in a given domain seem to be very fast at first but always seem to level off. This would seem to be very true. I feel like we can observe this phenomena everywhere in life. The better one becomes at something, the harder it is for them to improve at said thing. If you place a hot object next to a coolant, the object will cool rapidly at first, but this effect will only slow down until it has the same temperature as the coolant. Radioactive matter functions similarly to this once again, except with a half life, never really dying out fully. Conversely, other things seem to only grow faster and faster as time goes on. Think of a virus which has taken hold of a sickly person. I imagine the illness progressing slowly at first, but the weaker they become, the less they can defend themselves against the virus. Eventually, the invader wins and the person dies. I imagine that our sun will eventually die in quite the same way. Its end will be spectacular. 

From my observation, it would seem that many different things have many different curves, and it is seemingly impossible to give any sort of encompassing statement to determine which things will have which curves. This is just another mystery. What I find interesting, however, is the fact that two things which are seemingly completely tied together can have entirely different curves. The technological improvements in any given domain may seem to flatten out, but technological improvement as a whole only seems to be getting faster and faster. This is a truly strange behavior that I cannot explain.
